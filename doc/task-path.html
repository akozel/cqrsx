Task path: from the outbox to the handler

What happens to a task after its command committed it to the outbox with its event, until its handler has done the work: the claim that leases it, the buffer and the workers, the budget check before the handler, the outcome of the attempt and its settlement, the pause the task type's policy chooses, and the recovery of leases that ran out.

Guarantee
At least once: a committed task is delivered until it is done, fails, or runs out of attempts. A handler must be safe to repeat.
Lease
A claim leases the task to one process, with a fresh token and an expiry by the database's clock. Every settlement is checked against both.
Retry by policy
The handler answers done, retry or fail; the pause before the next attempt and the number of attempts belong to the policy of the task's type.

Overall diagram

Every process of the engine runs one TaskRuntime over the outbox table, transactional_outbox, through PostgresTaskQueue (or the queue in memory). The runtime has one producer that claims tasks into a bounded buffer and a fixed pool of workers that take them from it. Claim, settlement and recovery are one autocommitted statement each.

Queue
TaskQueue: claim, settle, recover_expired. PostgresTaskQueue runs each as one statement under the pool's statement timeout; MemoryTaskQueue mirrors it in memory.
Runtime
TaskRuntime, built by prepare(queue, dispatcher, config, policies) and run until its token is cancelled. Its limits are TaskRuntimeConfig: the workers C, the buffer Q, the poll, the lease, the handler and storage timeouts.
Executor
DefaultTaskExecutor: one leased attempt within the lease's deadlines, its settlement in a separate statement. It catches a handler's panic.
Dispatcher
TaskDispatcher, one for every worker: routes a task's format to its adapter and gives the handler its TaskContext (the attempt, the retry pause, the dependencies, the command executors).
Handler
A TaskHandler<T>, usually a function with #[task_handler]: the decoded task, the context and dependencies by type; it answers TaskResult::Done, Retry { reason } or Fail { reason }.
Policies
Policies binds each task type to a named TaskPolicy: a RetryPause and an attempt budget. The planner of the reactions reads the budget, the executor the pause.

Life of a task

The status column carries a task through four states. Only processing rows have an owner, a token and a lease (a check constraint of the table says so), and only completed and failed rows have finished_at.

The booking from the command path committed the task encore_record_ticket. Its handler, record_ticket, records the ticket in the fan's ledger with a command, whose event in turn plans encore_send_e_ticket. The demo runs 2 workers with a buffer of 2, polls every 300 ms and leases for 30 s; the library's defaults are larger (128, 128, 1 s, 60 s).

Pause by policy

A handler never names a pause. It answers Retry, and the executor waits the pause of the policy its task type is bound to. A policy has a name the application chooses (PolicyName, a constant), and a value from its configuration: RetryPause::fixed(pause), or RetryPause::backoff(base, max), which waits min(base · 2n−1, max) after attempt n, without jitter; and an attempt budget. In the engine a module defines it with Module::policy(name, pause, attempts) and binds a handler's type with .task(handler).policy(name); the reactions that plan the task never name it.

  • A type without a policy gets DEFAULT_MAX_ATTEMPTS (10) and the queue's own redelivery_backoff: 5 s · 2n−1, at most 300 s, each pause drawn ±20 %.
  • Timeouts and expired leases always wait the redelivery backoff, whatever the policy: the handler did not answer.
  • The budget counts claims. An attempt is counted when the task is claimed, so a lease that ran out or a timeout spends an attempt like an answered retry. A handler that always panics spends the budget and the task fails.
  • In the demo the steps of a chain (recording a ticket or a cancellation) wait a fixed 1 s, the e-mails back off from 1 to 8 s, and both have 6 attempts (ENCORE_TASK_MAX_ATTEMPTS, ENCORE_TASK_RETRY_BASE_MS, ENCORE_TASK_RETRY_MAX_MS).

The lease budget

A task's lease starts at the claim, not when a worker takes it: time in the buffer is spent from the lease. So the runtime refuses to start unless the lease covers the worst healthy case, checked by TaskRuntimeConfig::try_new: a claim and a settlement (two storage timeouts S), the handler (H), the margin, and every wave of the buffer ahead of the task, ⌈Q / C⌉ times a handler and a settlement.

Rulelease > 2·S + H + 5 s + ⌈Q/C⌉·(H + S)validate_for_storage_timeout

Demo30 s > 24 sS 3 s, H 5 s, Q = C = 2: 6 + 5 + 5 + 8

A command from a handler waits for its aggregate's lock; the engine therefore refuses a lock wait (COMMAND_LOCK_MAX_WAIT_MS) that is not shorter than the handler timeout (OUTBOX_HANDLER_TIMEOUT_MS).

Failed tasks

A task fails when its handler answers Fail, when a retry or an expired lease comes on its last attempt, or when its row cannot be delivered at all. A failure is logged once, with the task's id, format, attempt and cause (permanent failure, retry limit reached, worker lease expired), never the payload; the row keeps last_error. Nothing retries it automatically. An operator who has fixed the cause runs REQUEUE_FAILED_OUTBOX_TASK (cqrsx-postgres/sql/requeue-failed-outbox.sql) with the task's id: the row returns to pending with its attempts at zero and keeps its id and payload, so a receiver that deduplicates by the task id still recognises it. The operations section of cqrsx-engine describes it.

Guarantees

The numbers refer to guarantees.md.

Guaranteed

Published with the event G-06, G-15
A task exists exactly when the event that planned it was committed.
At least once G-16
A task is delivered again after a lost acknowledgement, an expired lease or a crash, until it is done, fails or runs out of attempts.
One owner per lease G-17, G-20
A claim locks the rows it takes and skips the locked ones; a settlement applies only with the lease's token and before its expiry, one task per statement.
Bounded attempts and pauses G-18, G-19
Lease, poll, handler and storage timeouts are checked together at start; retries follow the policy of the type within its budget.
A dead worker is replaced G-21
A worker that dies outside a handler is restarted with the same dispatcher; on stop, started attempts finish and settle.
Causation continues G-13, G-14
A task carries the ids of the command that planned it; a command from its handler keeps the correlation id and names the task as its cause.

Not guaranteed

Exactly-once effects G-16, G-22
A handler may run again after it did its work. It decides from the current state whether its step still applies; a command it issues may answer "already done".
Order
No order is promised between tasks, not even of one aggregate: a claim takes the oldest ready rows of a format, and pauses reorder them.
Immediate start
A commit does not wake the task runtime; a new task waits for the next tick of some process (1 s by default, 300 ms in the demo).
A failure that heals itself
A failed task stays failed until an operator requeues it.
