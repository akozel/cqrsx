Read path: from the commit to the view

What happens to an event after its command committed and before the document of a view shows it: the projection processor that reads the journal from its checkpoint, the lanes that apply the events key by key, the checkpoint that follows the applied pages, and the lease that keeps one owner per view. What is guaranteed, where the weak spots are and how the path behaves under load.

Guarantee
An accepted command is already in the journal. A view catches up by itself: no event is lost and none is applied twice, even when a node or the view store goes down.
Latency
A commit wakes the processors of its own node at once (after the wake delay, if one is set); on another node the owner finds it at its next poll, every 250 ms by default.
One owner per view
One process applies a view at a time, under a lease. It works in parallel across keys and strictly in order within a key.

Overall diagram

Writing and reading are apart. The command executor answers as soon as the event is committed. A background task, the projection processor of the view, reads the journal of the view's aggregate type from its checkpoint and writes the documents. The engine runs one ProjectionProcessor per view and aggregate type in every process; only the holder of the checkpoint's lease works, the others wait. In the demo the views concert_halls and fan_wallets each have such a processor, in MongoDB.

Journal and position
Every event stores in tx the id of the transaction that wrote it (xid8, default pg_current_xact_id()): its Position in the journal of its aggregate type. Inside one aggregate the positions follow the sequence.
Wake-up
CommitWakeup, one per aggregate type, shared by its executor and its processors. A signal in memory without data; it never crosses processes.
Processor
ProjectionProcessor, configured by ProcessorConfig. The engine gives it the view's ViewProjection, reads the journal through a pool of its own (the projection pool, 4 connections by default) and runs it under ProjectionRuntime.
Lanes
The ordered delivery: a bounded buffer per busy key (DeliveryLimits: 64 jobs, batches of 32). A key has at most one lane, so the handler never runs twice at once for one key.
View projection
ViewProjection over a ViewRepository: MongoViewRepository, PostgresViewRepository or the one in memory. A document is the fold of View::apply over the events 1..=last_sequence of its aggregate.
Checkpoint
One row per view and aggregate type behind CheckpointStore (MongoCheckpointStore, PostgresCheckpointStore): position, delivered, the lease (owner, lease_token, lease_until), failures, last_error and the state of a rebuild.

In the background the owner extends its lease every third of it (every 3.3 s with the default 10 s lease) and, once per poll interval, takes the rebuild requests from its row. Processors without the lease try to claim the row once per poll interval; they get it only when the owner releases it or its lease runs out. When the journal has nothing new, on the first idle poll and every eighth after it the owner looks at the journal's head: a head above the read position means a slower transaction holds the horizon (Waiting::Horizon).

The journal's horizon

A transaction gets its id when it first writes, and transactions commit in any order. A reader that took everything visible could move its checkpoint past the event of a transaction that has not finished yet; when that transaction commits, its event lies behind the checkpoint and is never read. So a page stops at the horizon: the oldest transaction still running, pg_snapshot_xmin(pg_current_snapshot()) of the reading statement. Everything below it has finished, so there are no holes. The page's LIMIT solves another problem: it bounds the size of a page.

The price of the horizon: fresh events wait for the slowest writing transaction in the database, even one of another application. A session left idle in transaction stops every view until PostgreSQL ends it; the demo's compose file ends it after 30 seconds (idle_in_transaction_session_timeout=30s).

Failures and pauses

The checkpoint moves only behind the unbroken prefix of applied pages, so a failure stops it where it is. When a key fails, the owner stops reading, waits for every receipt still in flight, moves the checkpoint to the last transaction before the first one that touches the failed key, records the failure on the row (failures, last_error: a category and the handler's name, never a payload) and pauses. Then it clears its window and reads again from the checkpoint; the documents that already took the repeated events skip them. The pause is 0.5 s after the first failure in a row and doubles up to 10 s; an advance resets the count.

  • A gap heals in place. A document that is behind the delivered events (PersistenceError::SequenceGap) writes nothing; the owner queues a rebuild of that key behind the failed job, and the page waits for the rebuild as for the job. The round does not fail.
  • An event that does not decode cuts its page before its transaction and stops the reading there. The view stands at that place and repeats with the pause until the event can be read (an upcaster, a fix of the code).
  • A lost lease (another token on the row, or no successful extension before the local deadline) ends the ownership at once; the processor goes back to claiming.

Rebuilds

A rebuild replaces a document with the fold of its aggregate's whole history (RebuildableProjection::rebuild). It runs through the same lanes as the live events, so a rebuild and the live events of one key never run at once, and the writes of both share the limit of 16. There are three kinds: the whole view (CheckpointStore::request_rebuild with a pace between the events of one key; the owner walks the keys of the journal 100 at a time and keeps its cursor on the row, so another owner continues it), one key (request_rebuild_key), and a key healed after a gap. Writes go on during a rebuild. A document that is ahead of the history the rebuild read is not rolled back: the history is read again, up to three times. The processor registers its row before every claim, so a row dropped together with the view store comes back at the start of the journal, and its next owner rebuilds every document from there.

view::verify checks a view: every document against the fold of the part of its history it has applied; a document behind its journal is not an error.

Guarantees

The numbers refer to guarantees.md.

Guaranteed

No loss G-24
The checkpoint moves only behind the unbroken prefix of applied pages and stops before the transaction of a failed key; the horizon never skips an unfinished transaction; after any failure reading starts again from the checkpoint.
No double application G-25
A document is the fold of the unbroken prefix 1..=last_sequence: older events are skipped, a gap is refused before anything is written.
Order within a key G-24
One lane per key; its events are applied in journal order.
One writer of the checkpoint G-23
A lease with a token: a former owner cannot move the checkpoint. Documents are written at the revision they were read with, so of two writes of one revision only one passes.
Rebuilds without stopping writes G-26
A rebuild of the view, of a key or of a document with a gap ends in the fold of the history.
Commands do not wait for views G-09
The answer comes after the commit and the wake-up, not after the view. With the views in MongoDB, commands are accepted while MongoDB is down.
Verification G-28
A document equals the fold of the prefix it has applied.

Not guaranteed

Reading your own write
Right after the answer the document may still be old. A screen that must show the result waits for applied of its document (the demo: projection.applied).
Consistency between views
Each view has its own processor and checkpoint; two views of one event are updated independently, and a view fed by events born from tasks lags further.
A bound on the lag
If the owner cannot keep up, the lag grows; commands are not slowed down. Cqrs::views().lag(name) counts it up to the lag cap (100 000 by default, PROJECTION_LAG_CAP).
Order across keys
Documents of different aggregates are written in parallel and independently.

Load and scaling

  • A hot aggregate is bounded on the command path, by its lock, not by its view: the view of one key takes the events of many commands in one batch.
  • Inside the owner the work is parallel across keys (16 documents written at once, a window of 63 pages) and sequential within a key. A slow key slows itself: its buffer fills and its next page waits for space, while memory stays bounded.
  • The wake delay trades a little latency for fewer writes: the commits of a burst land in one page, so a document is read and written once per batch instead of once per commit.
  • More nodes add command capacity as long as the database has room, but not view capacity: a view has one owner. Splitting a view into ranges of keys with checkpoints of their own is not implemented.
  • Measurements of the demo under load are made with the stand in demo/load; this page states no numbers, since they belong to a stand and a version.
